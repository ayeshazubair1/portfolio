    Customer Concentration & Revenue Risk

    Tools PostgreSQL, Tableau
    Duration May 2026 - Jun 2026

    Business Context

    Contoso is a global retail company operating across 8 countries, with physical stores and an online store, offering multiple product categories. Revenue grew from $88M in 2015 to $444M in 2022 before declining, raising concerns around customer retention and revenue concentration. Leadership suspects that revenue may be heavily dependent on a relatively small group of high-value customers, creating potential exposure if these customers reduce engagement or churn.

    The Challenge: Assess dependency on high-value customers, identify early disengagement signals, and quantify revenue exposed to potential churn.

    Executive Summary

    Contoso shows meaningful customer concentration risk, with its top 20% of customers generating 43.5% of total revenue and 44.26% of total profit. Within this high-value segment, 1,421 customers are classified as Declining, representing $78.7M in historical revenue exposure and $44.2M in associated profit.

    The exposure is concentrated in a small number of channels and locations. The Online channel accounts for $27.1M across 1,420 at-risk customers, representing 34.4% of total at-risk revenue. A further $34.0M is concentrated across 18 high-risk physical stores.

    Customer segmentation also shows signs of weakening engagement: only 26% of high-value customers are Consistent, while approximately 49% are classified as Inactive. These findings point to a need for targeted retention efforts focused on declining high-value customers, the Online channel, and high-risk physical stores.

    Key Findings

    1. Nearly Half of Contoso's Revenue Flows from Just 20% of Customers

    • The top 20% of customers contribute 43.5% of total revenue and 44.26% of total profit.
    • Within that group, the top 10% alone account for more than a quarter of both revenue and profit.
    • This level of concentration increases the business's exposure if high-value customers reduce spending or churn.

    2. Within the Top 20%, Engagement Is Fragmenting

    • Only 26% of high-value customers show consistent engagement, while 49% are classified as Inactive.
    • The Declining segment (1,421 customers) has an average of 303 days since its last recorded purchase, despite historically healthy purchase frequency.
    • This suggests that revenue risk is emerging before customers are fully lost, creating an opportunity for targeted retention efforts.

    3. $78.7M in Historical Revenue Is Associated with Declining High-Value Customers

    • The Declining segment represents $78.7M in historical revenue and $44.2M in associated profit, making it the priority segment for retention efforts.
    • In comparison, Inactive high-value customers account for $411M in historical revenue, highlighting the scale of historical customer value within this segment.
    • Prioritizing Declining customers creates an opportunity to investigate disengagement and address retention risks before they become more pronounced.

    4. Risk Is Concentrated — One Channel Dominates Revenue Exposure

    • The Online channel holds $27.1M at risk across 1,420 declining customers, representing 34% of total at-risk revenue.
    • Eighteen high-risk physical stores contribute an additional $34M, but the exposure is distributed across locations rather than concentrated in a single channel.
    • Targeting the Online channel and high-risk stores is likely to produce a greater impact than broad company-wide retention initiatives.

    Recommendations

    1. Retain Declining High-Value Customers (Immediate)

    Target Segment: 1,421 Declining customers ($78.7M revenue at risk)

    • Prioritize customers with the longest inactivity periods
    • Personalize offers using historical purchase behavior
    • Collect feedback to identify drivers of disengagement

    2. Treat Online Channel Risk (Critical)

    Target Area: Online channel ($27.1M at-risk revenue)

    • Segment outreach by inactivity duration
    • Prioritize high-spend customers for direct intervention
    • Implement dedicated digital retention campaigns

    3. Activate Store-Level Retention in High-Risk Locations

    Target Area: 18 high-risk stores ($34M at-risk revenue)

    • Provide store-level lists of at-risk customers to each store manager
    • Review purchase history for product availability issues
    • Track monthly customer reactivation and recovered revenue by store

    4. Raise Customer Concentration Risk to Leadership

    Target Area: Customer concentration risk

    • Establish monitoring thresholds for top-customer activity
    • Reduce long-term dependency through broader customer acquisition

    Limitations & Assumptions

    1. Partial 2024 Data: Customer recency is measured against the latest sales record (April 20, 2024), limiting the assessment of engagement beyond this date.
    2. Churn Estimated from Behavior: At-risk customers were identified using recency and frequency patterns, not confirmed churn records.
    3. Multi-Currency Transactions: Revenue figures were calculated in transaction currency without USD normalization. Post-analysis validation found a 1.47% net difference, indicating limited currency-related impact on the overall findings.
    4. Threshold Benchmarks: All segmentation thresholds were derived from internal data distributions, as no external industry benchmarks were available.
    5. No External Context: The analysis identifies who is at risk and how much revenue is exposed, but cannot fully explain why customers are disengaging without feedback or market data.
    6. Synthetic Dataset: Contoso is a fictional dataset. Additional context such as loyalty history, customer feedback, or campaign data was unavailable.
